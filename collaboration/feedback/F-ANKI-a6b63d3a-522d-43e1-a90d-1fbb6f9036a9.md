## Finding and rationale
The initial object types include flashcards, the feed ranks by due status, and the MVP changes later ranking from answers. Yet Anki scheduling authority is an unresolved experiment. Without an interim boundary, Paideia may create a second review history for cards that also exist in Anki, even before “full Anki embedding.” The spec rightly forbids two schedulers but does not say whether MVP flashcards are Paideia-only, Anki-linked read-only views, or excluded until the authority decision.

## Evidence (URLs, observed tests, or explicit unknowns)
Internal: section 7 lists flashcards and due status; section 9 says Anki remains authoritative until explicit migration and “Do not grade a card in two schedulers”; section 13 includes answer logging/ranking and a separate Anki experiment. No local integration or scheduling test has occurred.

## Proposed change and alternatives
Define card origin, stable external mapping, review authority, and write permissions for each MVP card state. The smallest safe pilot may exclude existing Anki cards from Paideia grading and use separate synthetic Paideia questions. Alternatively, make Anki-linked cards display-only until the disposable-collection experiment proves a single authoritative review path. Keep the owner’s Anki objective intact.

## Implementation impact and acceptance test
Adds a contract before card/event work. In a disposable collection, answer and retry a mapped card from both clients; show one authoritative scheduling transition and an explainable mapping of Paideia events to Anki review history, or demonstrate that the Paideia control is unavailable while authority is unresolved.
