## Finding and rationale
The spec treats quotation verification, source-backed explanation, and answer feedback as one content pipeline, but they need different validation. A substring or normalized exact-match check can establish whether quoted text appears in an extracted passage; it cannot establish that an inference follows, a question has a unique answer, or feedback is pedagogically valid. “Generated content has a source” also permits an answer to cite a passage that does not support it.

## Evidence (URLs, observed tests, or explicit unknowns)
Internal: section 7 proposes exact-match quotation checks and validation state; section 12 asks reviewers to compare claims and alternate answers; section 13 gates on a verified quote, question, and source-citing answer feedback. The baseline does not specify separate pass criteria for these classes. No empirical accuracy claim is made here.

## Proposed change and alternatives
Define a validation matrix by object type: quotation fidelity and locator; extraction/OCR uncertainty; claim support; answer/rubric sufficiency; plausible alternate answers; and generation provenance. A source link is necessary but not sufficient for a “verified” label. For the first slice, manual approval of a small set of questions with a recorded reason may be simpler than automating claim validation.

## Implementation impact and acceptance test
Adds validation states and review effort, likely limits initial content volume. Use synthetic passages with a correct quote, altered quote, unsupported inference, ambiguous question, and valid alternate answer. The pipeline must label each distinctly and withhold invalid or unresolved objects from the feed.
